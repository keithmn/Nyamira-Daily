Three things this note does not tell you

  • Borrowing here is net. The KSh 1.15 trillion deficit is new loans after old loans are repaid. Repaying foreign loans alone takes KSh 412.9 billion more (April estimates).
  • A plan, not spending. These are budget-day figures. What was actually spent comes later from the Controller of Budget, and is usually less.
  • Counties get more than this slice. The equitable share is KSh 428 billion; with conditional grants counties get KSh 502 billion, part of it counted inside ministries.

Same budget, three numbers

How much does education get?

You will see three different answers in the news. All three can be right: each comes from a different document, a different stage or a different grouping.

Our rule: we always say which document and which stage a number comes from, and we never compare numbers from different groupings.

Chapter 4 · Counties

How the 47 counties' share is decided, and what yours gets

Counties' share is set in three steps: the Commission on Revenue Allocation recommends, the Treasury proposes, and Parliament decides in the Division of Revenue Act. Then a formula splits it among counties.

The formula

How CRA's recommended share is split between counties for FY2026/27 (the fourth basis).

Every county gets the same basic share; the rest follows population, poverty and land area. Some money is also held as a baseline from the previous year, so a county's total is not the formula alone.

CRA recommendation, FY2026/27Share in FY2025/26

Parliament set the final total at KSh 428 billion, KSh 30.9 billion below CRA's recommendation. Each county's amount in the final County Allocation of Revenue Act will be added when we have checked the Act itself; we do not scale the recommendation down to guess it.

When you can still be heard
